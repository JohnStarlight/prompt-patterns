# Prompt Transformation with Patterns

## Prompt 1 (Simple): Write about climate change.

### Answer: 
Climate change refers to long-term shifts in temperatures and weather patterns. These shifts may be natural, but since the 1800s, human activities have been the main driver of climate change, primarily due to the burning of fossil fuels like coal, oil, and gas, which produces heat-trapping gases. 

The consequences of climate change now include, among others, intense droughts, water scarcity, severe fires, rising sea levels, flooding, melting polar ice, catastrophic storms and declining biodiversity.

## Prompt 2 (Instructional): Write a 200-word explanation of climate change for high school learners. Focus on the greenhouse effect and human impact.

### Answer:
Think of Earth like a car parked in the sun with the windows rolled up. The glass lets light in but traps the heat inside. This is essentially the "Greenhouse Effect." Naturally, our atmosphere has gases like CO2 that act like that glass, keeping Earth warm enough for life. 

However, since the Industrial Revolution, humans have been "cranking up the heat." By burning fossil fuels for cars and electricity, we’ve pumped massive amounts of extra CO2 into the atmosphere. We are essentially thickening the glass of the greenhouse. 

For high schoolers today, this isn't just a science project; it’s a global shift. The extra heat energy leads to more volatile weather, melting glaciers, and rising sea levels. While the planet has changed before, it has never changed this fast during human history. The challenge for your generation lies in "decarbonizing" our economy—finding ways to power our lives without adding more layers to that atmospheric blanket.

## Prompt 3 (Comparative): Compare the effects of climate change on coastal vs inland regions in 5 bullet points.

### Answer:
* **Primary Threat:** Coastal regions face immediate risks from sea-level rise and storm surges, while inland regions are more vulnerable to prolonged droughts and extreme heatwaves.
* **Economic Impact:** Coastal economies often suffer from the destruction of high-value real estate and shipping infrastructure; inland economies face crop failures and decreased agricultural productivity.
* **Water Security:** Coastal areas struggle with saltwater intrusion into freshwater aquifers, whereas inland areas deal with the drying up of lakes, rivers, and mountain snowpacks.
* **Migration Patterns:** Coastal populations are more likely to experience "managed retreat" or forced displacement due to flooding, while inland populations may migrate due to resource scarcity or uninhabitable temperatures.
* **Ecosystem Shifts:** Coastal change is defined by the loss of salt marshes and mangroves; inland change is marked by shifting forest boundaries and an increased frequency of wildfires.

Basically, clear prompts save you from "the fluff." By being specific, you stop the AI from guessing what you want and start getting answers you can actually use right away.
